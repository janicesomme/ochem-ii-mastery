Same joining move.
Now start with a ketone.

Keep what is already attached. Add the incoming carbon group at the same site.

One new starting pattern

You already know how methyl and ethyl join an aldehyde. Now we will use those same reagents with a ketone.

Draw the product connections on paper before revealing each answer. The new information comes after two familiar questions.

1 · Two familiar aldehydes

QUESTION 1

Add methyl. Draw the product connections.

Help me find the move

Find the aldehyde carbon and add the carbon supplied by CH₃MgBr.

Show the skeletal answer

Add methyl at the aldehyde carbon and finish with OH there. That carbon now has two carbon neighbors, so the product is a secondary alcohol.

QUESTION 2

Add ethyl. Keep its two carbons together.

Help me find the move

The carbon holding MgBr makes the new bond. Its partner carbon comes with it.

Show the skeletal answer

The ethyl group joins at the aldehyde carbon. After water, you have a secondary alcohol. The OH carbon has two carbon neighbors.

2 · The ketone already has two carbon groups

Find the carbon double-bonded to O. This time it has a carbon group on each side. That makes it a ketone.

Keep both groups. Add the incoming group at that same carbon. After water, finish with OH there.

The black carbon–carbon bonds were already there. The amber bond joins the new methyl group.

Count only the carbon neighbors of the OH carbon

Two existing carbon neighbors + one new carbon neighbor = three. An alcohol whose OH carbon has three carbon neighbors is a tertiary alcohol.

Do not replace either original group. You are adding a third carbon group, not swapping one for another.

HO and OH show the same alcohol group. Here the label is turned around so the O sits next to its bond.

3 · Repeat the ketone move

QUESTION 3

Add methyl to this ketone.

Help me find the move

Keep the carbon group on each side of the carbon double-bonded to O. Add one new methyl group at that same carbon.

Show the skeletal answer

Keep both original methyl groups. Add the third methyl group and finish with OH. The OH carbon has three carbon neighbors: this is a tertiary alcohol.

QUESTION 4

Same addition, longer groups on the ketone.

Help me find the move

The groups on the left and right are longer, but both stay attached. Where does the new methyl go?

Show the skeletal answer

Put methyl on the carbon that was double-bonded to O. Keep both existing ethyl groups and finish with OH. The product is still a tertiary alcohol.

QUESTION 5

Repeat it once more.

Help me find the move

Do not count the whole chain to classify the alcohol. Look only at the carbon carrying OH and its direct carbon neighbors.

Show the skeletal answer

The original two propyl groups stay attached. Add methyl at the same carbon and finish with OH. There are three direct carbon neighbors, so this is a tertiary alcohol.

4 · Same reagent, aldehyde or ketone?

QUESTION 6A

A: add ethyl to the aldehyde.

Help me find the move

Before the addition, the aldehyde carbon has one carbon neighbor and one H.

Show the skeletal answer

Add the ethyl group and finish with OH. The OH carbon now has two carbon neighbors: secondary alcohol.

QUESTION 6B

B: add ethyl to the ketone.

Help me find the move

Before the addition, the ketone carbon already has two carbon neighbors.

Show the skeletal answer

Add the same ethyl group and finish with OH. Keep both carbon groups that were already there. The OH carbon now has three carbon neighbors: tertiary alcohol.

5 · Change the question format

QUESTION 7

Draw the product connections.

Help me find the move

Ethyl is one connected two-carbon piece. Add it to the ketone carbon without removing either existing methyl group.

Show the skeletal answer

The OH carbon ends with two methyl groups and one ethyl group. It has three direct carbon neighbors, even though the ethyl branch contains two carbons.

Show the skeletal answer

Choose CH₃MgBr. The new group is methyl: one added carbon. Keep the water step after the addition.

Same move. One extra group already there.

These aldehydes: one carbon neighbor before addition, two after → secondary alcohol.

These ketones: two carbon neighbors before addition, three after → tertiary alcohol.

Count the carbons directly attached to the OH carbon, not every carbon in the molecule.

Keep the scope clear

These are Grignard additions, not NaBH₄ reductions. NaBH₄ adds H at this site; a Grignard reagent adds a carbon group.

The ketones here have matching groups on both sides, so the new alcohol carbon is not a stereocenter. The aldehyde recap examples also give matching carbon groups. Other examples may require a stereochemistry decision.

Use dry ether for the Grignard step, then water afterward. Competing groups and protecting groups belong to a later worksheet.
