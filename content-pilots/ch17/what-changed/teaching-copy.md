# What changed? Does it matter? — editable copy

## Pair 1: A longer chain

**Answer:** Same reaction rule

**What changed:** B has a longer carbon chain. Both ends are still aldehydes, and both use NaBH₄.

An aldehyde has a carbon double-bonded to O and also bonded to H. NaBH₄ turns that end into a primary alcohol. The longer chain stays longer—you use the same move at its end.

**Use this next time:** Check the reacting group before worrying about chain length. Same rule does not mean the two whole products are identical.

## Pair 2: The drawing points the other way

**Answer:** Same reaction rule—and the same molecule

**What changed:** B is the same structure turned over on the page. Its connections have not changed.

Both drawings show a ketone: the carbon double-bonded to O has a carbon group on each side. NaBH₄ gives the same secondary alcohol. Page direction does not change the reaction.

**Use this next time:** Read the connections, not whether the oxygen points up or down. These plain bonds do not specify a different stereoisomer.

## Pair 3: Two rings join the picture

**Answer:** Same reaction rule

**What changed:** B has two rings attached to the chain. The reacting end is still an aldehyde.

NaBH₄ turns the aldehyde into a primary alcohol in both examples. The rings shown here stay unchanged. Keep the complete skeleton when drawing each product.

**Use this next time:** A larger skeleton is not automatically a new reaction. Still scan its other groups before deciding they stay unchanged.

## Pair 4: A different reagent name

**Answer:** Same product here

**What changed:** A uses NaBH₄ in methanol. B uses LiAlH₄, followed by water.

Both sets of conditions reduce this aldehyde to the same primary alcohol. LiAlH₄ reacts with more types of groups, but this simple aldehyde gives the same product.

**Use this next time:** These reagents are not interchangeable in every question. Decide what they do to the particular group in front of you. Never use methanol as the LiAlH₄ reaction solvent; the water step comes afterward.

## Pair 5: Aldehyde or ketone?

**Answer:** Same bond-changing move; different alcohol type

**What changed:** A has H attached to the carbon double-bonded to O. B has carbon groups on both sides of that carbon.

NaBH₄ turns both into alcohols. The aldehyde gives a primary alcohol: its OH carbon has one carbon neighbor. The ketone gives a secondary alcohol: its OH carbon has two carbon neighbors.

**Use this next time:** Use the same reduction move, but keep the carbon connections. They tell you which alcohol type you made.

## Pair 6: One extra oxygen matters

**Answer:** Different outcome: only A reacts

**What changed:** In B, the carbon double-bonded to O is also bonded to another O that joins a carbon group. That makes B an ester.

NaBH₄ turns A’s ketone into an alcohol. It leaves B’s ester unchanged under these conditions. Finding the double bond to oxygen is only the first check.

**Use this next time:** Look at what else is attached to the carbon double-bonded to O. That extra oxygen changes which rule applies.

## Pair 7: Now the reagent change matters

**Answer:** Different outcome: only B reacts

**What changed:** The starting ester is identical. Only the reagent conditions change.

NaBH₄ leaves this ester unchanged. LiAlH₄, followed by water, turns it into two alcohol pieces. The carbonyl side becomes a primary alcohol; the oxygen-linked methyl piece becomes methanol.

**Use this next time:** Pair the reagent with the starting group. The reagent swap made no product difference for the aldehyde in pair 4, but it makes a big difference for this ester.
