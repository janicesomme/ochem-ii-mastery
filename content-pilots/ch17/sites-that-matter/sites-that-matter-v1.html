Sites That Matter

Find the few places that change. Keep the rest of the drawing in view while you check.

Amber outline: the site involved in this step, or its changed product site. Gray dashed outline: a look-alike group to check that stays unchanged in this step. These outlines do not show electron charge.

Select a button to compare the original drawing with highlighted sites. On a narrow screen, scroll the diagram sideways.

1. Two C=O groups. Only one changes.

Start with NaBH₄. It changes the ketone into an alcohol. The ester stays as it is.

Choose a step above to highlight its sites.

Quick check: Which clue tells you the other C=O belongs to an ester?

Its carbon is also bonded to an oxygen that joins another carbon group. That extra oxygen changes which reagent works.

2. Same molecule. Change the reagent, then look again.

In (a), two sites change. In (b), the ester also reacts—and the molecule separates into two pieces.

Quick check: Does the OH already on the ring mean that site has reacted in (a)?

No. It was already there. Compare the starting structure with the product before counting new OH groups.

3. A large molecule. Three familiar steps.

Follow the same upper site through the first two steps. Before the final step, scan the whole molecule again.

Choose a step above to highlight its sites.

Quick check: Why must you look again before the ozone step?

The previous step added another C=C. Looking only at the original molecule would miss one of the two sites that ozone changes.
