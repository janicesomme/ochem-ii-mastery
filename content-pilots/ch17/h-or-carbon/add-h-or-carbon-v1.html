NO FEAR OCHEM · CHAPTER 17 · V1

Add H—or add a carbon group?

Same starting site. Two different moves. Read the reagent before you draw.

1 · Learn the difference

Both reactions below start from the same aldehyde. Find the carbon with a double bond to O and a bond to H.

NaBH₄: no new carbon

NaBH₄ turns this aldehyde into a primary alcohol. Change the double bond to oxygen into a single bond. Add H to oxygen and to that same carbon.

Carbon check: two before, two after.

CH₃MgBr, then water: add one carbon

CH₃MgBr is a Grignard reagent: it supplies a carbon group that can join the aldehyde carbon. Here that group is methyl—one carbon.

Draw a new bond from the aldehyde carbon to a new line end. After the water step, its oxygen is OH. The amber bond shows where the new carbon joined.

Carbon check: two before, three after.

The difference to remember: NaBH₄ adds H at this carbon. CH₃MgBr adds a methyl group at this carbon. Both routes end with an alcohol, but only one adds carbon.

An unlabeled line end is carbon. Most H atoms on carbon are left unwritten. Amber marks the changed site or new bond; it does not represent charge.

Carry this into the next question

  1. Find the aldehyde. Its carbon is double-bonded to O and also bonded to H.
  2. Read the reagent. Does it supply H, or a carbon group?
  3. Check the product’s carbon count. NaBH₄ adds no carbon. CH₃MgBr adds one methyl carbon.

The drawings can change, and the question can hide a different piece. You still make the same decision.

Two boundaries to keep with this rule

These examples use aldehydes. Esters and other starting groups need their own rules.

In a Grignard reaction, water comes after the carbon-adding step. Water or an exposed OH group can use up the Grignard reagent before it joins the aldehyde. The Grignard steps here assume dry ether conditions before the water step.

What about wedges and dashes?

This first sheet asks for the product’s connections. When the methyl addition creates a carbon with four different groups, these conditions give an equal mixture of two mirror-image alcohols. The plain skeleton shows their shared connections. If a question asks for stereochemistry, draw both forms. The two-carbon aldehyde example gives matching methyl groups, so it does not create that stereocenter.
