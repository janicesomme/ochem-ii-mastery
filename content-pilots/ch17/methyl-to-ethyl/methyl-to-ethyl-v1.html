NO FEAR OCHEM · CHAPTER 17 · V1

Same move.
A bigger carbon piece.

You already know how to add methyl. Now keep the same joining move and bring two carbons instead of one.

The rule stays small

Attach the carbon holding MgBr to the aldehyde carbon. Keep the rest of the incoming piece together.

This sheet asks for product connections. Draw on paper first; then open the skeletal answer.

1 · Two familiar methyl additions

QUESTION 1

Add the methyl group. Draw the product connections.

Help me find the joining carbon

The reagent has one carbon. Join that carbon to the aldehyde carbon.

Show the skeletal answer

Make one new carbon–carbon bond at the aldehyde carbon. After water, its oxygen is OH. Two starting carbons plus one incoming carbon give three carbons.

QUESTION 2

Repeat the methyl move.

Help me find the joining carbon

The starting chain is longer. The one-carbon group still joins at the aldehyde carbon.

Show the skeletal answer

Keep the original three carbons. Add the methyl carbon at the aldehyde site, then finish with OH there. The product has four carbons.

2 · Meet the ethyl piece

Methyl has one carbon. Ethyl has two carbons already joined together. The circled corner below is the carbon holding MgBr. The line end to its right is the other carbon.

Use the circled carbon to make the new bond. Its partner stays attached.

Watch it join

The amber line is the new bond. The black bond inside the incoming two-carbon piece was already there.

After the water step, the aldehyde’s oxygen is OH. Two starting carbons + two incoming carbons = four product carbons.

Do not draw two separate methyl additions. Ethyl arrives as one connected two-carbon piece.

3 · Repeat the ethyl move

QUESTION 3

Keep the two-carbon piece together.

Help me find the joining carbon

Find the carbon directly bonded to MgBr. That is the one that joins the aldehyde carbon.

Show the skeletal answer

Join the MgBr-holding carbon to the aldehyde carbon. Keep its existing bond to the other carbon. Two plus two gives four carbons in the product.

QUESTION 4

Same joining move, another aldehyde.

Help me find the joining carbon

Do not split the two-carbon piece. Only its bond to MgBr is replaced by the new bond to the aldehyde carbon.

Show the skeletal answer

The ethyl group stays intact as it joins the aldehyde carbon. Finish with OH at the original aldehyde site. Three plus two gives five carbons.

QUESTION 5

One more ethyl addition.

Help me find the joining carbon

Ignore the extra chain length for a moment. Find the aldehyde carbon and the carbon holding MgBr.

Show the skeletal answer

Connect those two carbons and keep all the other carbon–carbon bonds. After water, the original oxygen is OH. Four plus two gives six carbons.

4 · Same aldehyde, different incoming piece

QUESTION 6A

A: attach the one-carbon piece.

Help me find the joining carbon

How many carbons are in this reagent’s carbon group?

Show the skeletal answer

This reagent adds one carbon. The three-carbon aldehyde gives a four-carbon alcohol.

QUESTION 6B

B: attach the two-carbon piece.

Help me find the joining carbon

The aldehyde is identical to A. Only the incoming piece has changed.

Show the skeletal answer

This reagent adds two carbons together. The same three-carbon aldehyde now gives a five-carbon alcohol. The joining move has not changed.

5 · Try it without highlighting

QUESTION 7

Draw the product connections.

Help me find the joining carbon

Find the carbon bonded to MgBr. Attach that carbon to the aldehyde carbon and carry its partner carbon with it.

Show the skeletal answer

The two-carbon piece joins intact. Finish with OH at the old aldehyde site. Five plus two gives seven carbons.

QUESTION 8

Draw the methyl or ethyl Grignard reagent that supplies the missing piece.

Help me find the joining carbon

Look at the new side of the OH carbon. Is the added piece one carbon long, or two?

Show the skeletal answer

Choose the ethyl reagent shown. The first new carbon is directly beside the OH carbon; the second is the next line end. The reagent must bring both carbons together.

The check to remember

  1. Find the carbon directly bonded to MgBr.
  2. Join it to the aldehyde carbon.
  3. Keep the incoming carbon piece intact.
  4. After water, put OH at the original aldehyde site.

A bigger incoming piece does not mean a new joining rule.

Reading these drawings

An unlabeled corner or line end represents carbon. MgBr is a labeled group, so the bond ending at that label does not add another carbon. Most H atoms on carbon are left unwritten.

Amber circles track the joining carbon; amber lines show the new bond. They do not indicate charge.

Keep these conditions with the rule

These examples use aldehydes and Grignard reagents in dry ether, followed by water. Water belongs after the addition. Other starting groups and an exposed OH can change what happens.

If a question asks for stereochemistry

This first sheet asks which atoms are connected. When the new alcohol carbon has four different groups, these conditions give both mirror-image forms in equal amounts. Draw both if the question asks for stereochemistry. When its two carbon groups match, that carbon is not a stereocenter.
