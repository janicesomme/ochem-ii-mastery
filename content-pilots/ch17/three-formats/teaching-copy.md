# Same reaction, three question formats — editable copy

## An aldehyde becomes a primary alcohol.

Look for the carbon with both a double bond to O and a bond to H. That is the aldehyde site. NaBH₄ turns it into a primary alcohol: the carbon carrying OH is attached to just one other carbon.

## A ketone becomes a secondary alcohol.

Look for the carbon double-bonded to O with a carbon group on each side. That is a ketone. NaBH₄ turns it into a secondary alcohol: the carbon carrying OH stays attached to two other carbons.

## A1 — Draw the product.

**Hint:** Find the carbon that has both a double bond to O and a bond to H.

**Answer explanation:** NaBH₄ turns this aldehyde into a primary alcohol. Make the bond to oxygen single and put H on the oxygen. The same carbon also gains H. Keep the chain exactly as it was.

## A2 — One more: draw the product.

**Hint:** The chain is longer. Does the group at the end still fit the aldehyde pattern?

**Answer explanation:** Yes—the end is still an aldehyde, so the same move gives a primary alcohol. A longer chain does not change this reaction.

## K1 — Draw the product.

**Hint:** The carbon double-bonded to O has a carbon group on each side. That makes it a ketone.

**Answer explanation:** NaBH₄ turns this ketone into a secondary alcohol. Make the bond to oxygen single and put H on oxygen. The same carbon also gains H. Keep both carbon groups attached.

## K2 — One more: draw the product.

**Hint:** Ignore the chain length for a moment. How many carbon groups are attached to the carbon double-bonded to O?

**Answer explanation:** There are two carbon groups attached, so this is still a ketone. NaBH₄ gives a secondary alcohol. The longer chains stay in place.

## A3 — Choose a reagent and solvent.

**Hint:** Compare the two drawings. An aldehyde has become a primary alcohol, and no carbon has been added.

**Answer explanation:** NaBH₄ in methanol is one suitable choice. You already know this reaction—you are supplying what goes over the arrow this time. Other valid reduction conditions can also work.

## A4 — Draw the aldehyde that would give this alcohol.

**Hint:** Start at the alcohol end and undo the reaction. Keep the carbon chain.

**Answer explanation:** Draw the aldehyde shown. Turn the single bond to oxygen back into a double bond and remove the H from oxygen. That end carbon has one H in the starting aldehyde. The skeletal drawing leaves most carbon-bound H implicit.

## K3 — Choose a reagent and solvent.

**Hint:** A ketone has become a secondary alcohol. The carbon groups on either side are unchanged.

**Answer explanation:** NaBH₄ in methanol is one suitable choice here too. The question format changed, but the reaction pattern did not.

## K4 — Draw the ketone that would give this alcohol.

**Hint:** Find the carbon carrying OH. Keep both of its carbon groups and undo the alcohol-forming change.

**Answer explanation:** Draw the ketone shown. Restore the double bond to oxygen and remove the H from oxygen. The H added to the middle carbon during reduction is also absent in the ketone.

## B1 — Find the reacting site, then draw the product.

**Hint:** Scan for a carbon double-bonded to O and bonded to H. The rings do not stop that end group from being an aldehyde.

**Answer explanation:** The aldehyde at the far right becomes a primary alcohol. Both rings and the connecting chain stay unchanged under these conditions. The highlighted answer shows the same local move as A1 and A2.

## E1 — Does this molecule change?

**Hint:** Do not stop at the double bond to O. The same carbon is also bonded to another O, which joins a carbon group. That makes this an ester.

**Answer explanation:** No reaction under these conditions: NaBH₄ in methanol leaves this ester unchanged. The repeated starting structure below shows that no alcohol product forms. Seeing a double bond to oxygen is not enough—you must identify the group.
