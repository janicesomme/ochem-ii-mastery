# Same move: methyl to ethyl

Attach the carbon holding MgBr to the aldehyde carbon. Keep the incoming piece together.

Methyl brings one carbon. Ethyl brings two carbons already joined together. The new bond uses the carbon directly bonded to MgBr.

## 1: Add the methyl group. Draw the product connections.

**Hint:** The reagent has one carbon. Join that carbon to the aldehyde carbon.

**Answer:** Make one new carbon–carbon bond at the aldehyde carbon. After water, its oxygen is OH. Two starting carbons plus one incoming carbon give three carbons.

## 2: Repeat the methyl move.

**Hint:** The starting chain is longer. The one-carbon group still joins at the aldehyde carbon.

**Answer:** Keep the original three carbons. Add the methyl carbon at the aldehyde site, then finish with OH there. The product has four carbons.

## 3: Keep the two-carbon piece together.

**Hint:** Find the carbon directly bonded to MgBr. That is the one that joins the aldehyde carbon.

**Answer:** Join the MgBr-holding carbon to the aldehyde carbon. Keep its existing bond to the other carbon. Two plus two gives four carbons in the product.

## 4: Same joining move, another aldehyde.

**Hint:** Do not split the two-carbon piece. Only its bond to MgBr is replaced by the new bond to the aldehyde carbon.

**Answer:** The ethyl group stays intact as it joins the aldehyde carbon. Finish with OH at the original aldehyde site. Three plus two gives five carbons.

## 5: One more ethyl addition.

**Hint:** Ignore the extra chain length for a moment. Find the aldehyde carbon and the carbon holding MgBr.

**Answer:** Connect those two carbons and keep all the other carbon–carbon bonds. After water, the original oxygen is OH. Four plus two gives six carbons.

## 6A: A: attach the one-carbon piece.

**Hint:** How many carbons are in this reagent’s carbon group?

**Answer:** This reagent adds one carbon. The three-carbon aldehyde gives a four-carbon alcohol.

## 6B: B: attach the two-carbon piece.

**Hint:** The aldehyde is identical to A. Only the incoming piece has changed.

**Answer:** This reagent adds two carbons together. The same three-carbon aldehyde now gives a five-carbon alcohol. The joining move has not changed.

## 7: Draw the product connections.

**Hint:** Find the carbon bonded to MgBr. Attach that carbon to the aldehyde carbon and carry its partner carbon with it.

**Answer:** The two-carbon piece joins intact. Finish with OH at the old aldehyde site. Five plus two gives seven carbons.

## 8: Draw the methyl or ethyl Grignard reagent that supplies the missing piece.

**Hint:** Look at the new side of the OH carbon. Is the added piece one carbon long, or two?

**Answer:** Choose the ethyl reagent shown. The first new carbon is directly beside the OH carbon; the second is the next line end. The reagent must bring both carbons together.
